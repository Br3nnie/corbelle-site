One group. Many countries. Nobody sure who could change what.

International corporate finance group · offices across Europe

The problem

The group had grown country by country. Each office ran its own IT, its own licences and its own way of working. When a senior executive asked a simple question — who can see and change what? — nobody could answer it. Local admins in one country could edit another country's files. The board wanted comfort, fast.

What we did

Phase 1 — Independent assessment. An independent, outside view of the group's technology, people and risks, written for the board to act on.

Phase 2 — Partner selection. Ran a proper selection across three IT partners: clear requirements, back-to-back vendor sessions, and one recommendation the board could stand behind.

Governance. Mapped every admin account and role, and agreed "freedom within a framework": one minimum security standard for everyone, with each country free to choose its own tools — AI included — inside it.

Where it's got to

A single managed IT partner. A clear plan to cut admin roles from around 28 to 4. A yearly report the board can read: who has access to what, and why. Phase 3, an AI-ready CRM roadmap, is now on the table.

Case study

Quotes from the garden, not the kitchen table.

Fencing & landscaping firm · South East England · owner-led

The problem

The business is great on site and hates admin. Every survey meant going back later to work out materials, labour and margin, then typing up a quote. Customers were left chasing for a week for a price — and in the trades, a slow price is a lost job. Off-the-shelf apps had been tried before. None of them stuck.

What we did

We started from how the team already works: they walk the garden and talk. So we built that. Whoever does the survey says the job out loud ("12 metres of close-board, two gates, take the old one away"), and it comes back as a priced PDF quote using the firm's own day rates and materials. They check it, then send it by WhatsApp while still on site. Everyone on the team can see every quote; only the owner can change prices.

The result

No forms, no laptop, no evening admin. Quotes go out while the customer is still keen. We're now tracking turnaround and win rate.

From years leading data and technology teams inside financial services. Same patterns, every size of business.

Financial services · 400 staff

"Just get me the report." It wasn't a report problem.

A chief exec who couldn't get the numbers they wanted

Processthe real fault, not the data
Upstreamwhere it got fixed
Nomore coding around gaps
Read the story

Problem: The investment team said reporting took ages and couldn't slice the numbers the way they wanted. They asked the data team to "code around it".

What we found: The data existed. The process for collecting it had never been looked at again. The people entering data weren't the ones who felt the pain when it was wrong.

Lesson: Fix the process upstream. Every workaround you code is one more thing to maintain, and the urgent report usually isn't opened again a week later.

Financial services · reporting migration

Carrying everything over. Including the reports nobody read.

A move from an old reporting system to a new one

6 mthsunopened = should be retired
Allreports carried over anyway
Moreadmin, not less
Read the story

Problem: Hundreds of pixel-perfect reports. The plan was to move all of them, then decide what was needed.

What happened: Extra migration work, data issues and admin, and a new system that felt clunkier than the old one.

Lesson: Clear out by exception. Retire anything nobody has opened in six months, then move only what people actually use. It's now a standard step in every Corbelle audit.

A group choosing a new system for six production companies

Read the story

Problem: Each production company wanted something different from the new system.

What we did: Spoke to every one of them, recorded what they said, grouped it into themes and played it back: "your CFO said this, your COO said that."

Lesson: People back a decision when they can see their own words in it. This is still how every Corbelle Find stage starts.

Problem: A business buys a new CRM. Nobody plans how it'll be used. Before long the old spreadsheets are back, and one business needed one and a half extra people just to work around the new system.

Lesson: Decide what "being used" looks like before you sign, and remove the old way of working on day one. Software doesn't change behaviour. People do.
