Who sees the AI bill?

Usage costs, who owns them, and when AI in a core process gets expensive.

Brendan EllisNov 2026 · 2 min read

I asked a senior executive a simple question recently.

"When AI is built into your critical processes and the usage costs go up, who sees the bill?"

There was a pause.

"I don't."

They're not alone.

Cheap to try. Easy to forget.

Most AI tools start small. A few licences. A free tier. A card on someone's expenses.

Then it works. People use it more. Someone connects it to a process. Then another.

And usage-based pricing means the bill grows with every task, every document and every question.

Nobody decided to spend more. It just happened.

Three costs people miss

1. Usage, not seats. Many AI tools charge by how much you use them, not just how many people have access. Busy month, bigger bill.

2. Review time. AI output still needs checking. If someone spends twenty minutes checking what took the AI two, count the twenty.

3. Duplication. Three teams, three different AI tools, three subscriptions doing the same job.

When it gets expensive

Experimenting is cheap. The cost changes when AI moves from "helpful extra" to "the process doesn't work without it".

At that point you're exposed. Prices change. Usage grows. And switching off isn't an option anymore.

That's not a reason to avoid AI. It's a reason to know what you're signing up for.

Four simple rules

Name an owner. One person sees every AI bill, every month.

Know the unit cost. What does one quote, one summary or one report cost to produce with AI, including review time?

Set a standard. Agree which tools are approved. Teams can choose freely, within the list.

Check quarterly. Is each tool still earning its place?

AI can save a lot of time. Just make sure someone is watching what the time costs.
