Conversation analysis · 13 August 2026

What the transcript revealed.

Seven recurring ways businesses mistake visible symptoms for the real operational problem—and spend money in the wrong place.

01

The reported problem is rarely the real problem.

Clients often present a symptom, an assumed cause and a preferred solution in the same sentence. The useful work is to pull them apart and trace the issue upstream.

Transcript evidence: A problem visible at a sewer overflow could originate a kilometre away. Building storage at the visible failure point may leave the original cause untouched.
02

More data does not automatically produce better decisions.

Tens of thousands of monitors can create an enormous volume of information without answering what it means, who should act or whether the intervention worked.

SignalMeaningCauseDecisionAction
03

Businesses measure proxies rather than reality.

A metric can be technically correct but commercially wrong. When mileage was used as the proxy for utilisation, stationary vehicles pumping on-site all day appeared inactive.

Diagnostic question: Does the metric represent the outcome we care about—or merely something the system can count easily?
04

Technology often formalises a bad process.

A group CRM required roughly one-and-a-half extra roles to administer. Teams recreated spreadsheets because the official system did not reflect how work really happened.

Principle: Never automate, digitise or scale a process until you understand why it exists and whether it works.
05

Quick fixes create downstream liabilities.

A visible intervention may provide immediate relief while creating more assets, maintenance, cost and complexity. It is easier to approve because it is tangible—not necessarily because it is right.

06

Maintenance and optimisation are undervalued.

Preventative work is hard to defend because its return is something that did not happen. Avoiding modest annual maintenance can eventually create a far larger replacement programme.

Transcript evidence: The conversation contrasted approximately £50,000 a year of deferred system maintenance with a later $20 million remediation programme.
07

The missing capability is interpretation.

The recurring constraint is not simply data, software or expertise. It is the ability to connect an operational signal to its meaning, cause, decision, action and measurable result.

Commercial opportunity: Help leaders establish what is actually broken before they commit to technology, headcount or infrastructure.
