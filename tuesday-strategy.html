AI

You don't need an AI strategy. You need a Tuesday strategy.

Why the boring wins pay for the shiny ones.

Brendan EllisNov 2026 · 2 min read

"We need an AI strategy."

I hear it every week. From boards. From investors. From owners who've just come back from a conference.

Here's my honest answer: you probably don't.

Not yet.

Strategies are written for a Monday

A strategy deck gets presented on a Monday. Big ambitions. Transformation. A roadmap with arrows.

Then Tuesday arrives.

Someone is still re-typing orders. Someone is still chasing invoices. Someone is still writing the weekly report by hand.

The deck didn't touch any of it.

What a Tuesday strategy looks like

A Tuesday strategy asks one question:

On a normal day, what are our people doing that they shouldn't have to?

Then it picks the three answers that cost the most, and fixes those first.

  • Drafting the same email twenty times a week
  • Summarising meetings nobody remembers
  • Turning site notes into quotes
  • Pulling numbers from three places into one report

Boring? Absolutely.

Valuable? More than most "AI strategies" I've seen.

Why boring wins first

Most AI projects never pay back. It's not a technology problem. It's a people and process problem.

Big, shiny projects fail because nobody changed how the work actually gets done. Small, boring wins succeed because the people doing the work can see the difference by Friday.

And when they can see it, they want more. That's how adoption actually happens. Not through a launch email. Through relief.

Three steps for this week

1. Ask, don't tell. Sit with the people doing the work and ask what eats their week.

2. Fix the process first. If a job shouldn't exist, don't automate it. Delete it.

3. Set one simple rule. What data can go into AI tools and what can't. One page. Not a policy nobody reads.

Then pick one job and make it disappear.

Do that a few times, and you'll have something better than an AI strategy.

You'll have proof.
